The problem

Three things visual grading can't see — and buyers can.

Field sorting catches surface damage. Tap-testing catches some maturity. Neither reaches what's happening inside the fruit — and that's exactly where loads get rejected.

01 / DEFECTS

Internal defects you cannot see

Hollow heart, internal cracking, and immature fruit look identical to premium melons from the outside. Every season, some percentage of your loads reaches retail with fruit that disappoints shoppers and triggers chargebacks.

02 / REJECTIONS

Rejections you cannot defend

When a buyer rejects a load and USDA runs a destination inspection, the producer with pre-shipment data walks in with evidence. The producer without it accepts whatever the inspection reports — and next year's offtake quietly shrinks.

03 / VARIABILITY

QC that varies by shift and person

The same load can be graded differently by two people at the same station. That variability isn't just a cost problem — it's a quality problem, and it makes buyer trust harder to hold.

2–5% typical defect rate ~2% truckload rejection rate Re-routing, freight loss & reduced next-season allocation

What it does

Non-destructive internal-quality grading, at industrial line speed.

Genus Echo sits inline on a single packing line. Watermelons pass through the scanner on your existing conveyor. The MRI captures a full internal image of each fruit; AI grading interprets it and outputs a quality score, defect classification, and a per-fruit data record.

How it works

MRI, then AI — biology turned into data.

Magnetic Resonance Imaging reads the behavior of hydrogen nuclei in the fruit. Water and tissue produce distinct signals the system uses to build a full internal image — no contact, no damage. Orbem's models, trained on millions of scanned units, then classify defects, measure maturity, and output structured data for every fruit in milliseconds.
